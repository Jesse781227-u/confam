Before you forward it, Confam it.

Confam is an AI-powered tool that helps INEC fight election misinformation online. Voters tag it under any post on X, Instagram or Facebook, or message it on WhatsApp, and it replies with what INEC has actually published, names the source, and answers in English, Pidgin, Yorùbá, Hausa or Igbo, by text or voice note. Every question it receives also shows INEC which false claims are spreading, so the Commission can respond early.

Every answer carries one of three stamps.

Confam never answers without saying how sure it is, and never states a fact without showing where it came from.

ConfirmedConfam

An official source backs it

Confam confirms the claim and links the INEC publication it matches, with the date it was published.

An official source contradicts it

Confam corrects the claim in the same thread, where the people who saw the lie will see the correction.

From a tag to a sourced answer.

The same AI engine sits behind every app. It can only answer from sources it has been given, it knows when to stop and ask a person, and it reports the claims it sees back to INEC.

Step 1

Someone asks

A voter tags Confam under a post, comments on its page, or sends a message, screenshot or voice note.

Step 2

Confam reads the claim

It pulls the text out of images, transcribes voice notes and works out which language to answer in.

Step 3

It looks for a source

It searches INEC publications and published fact-checks, and checks images for signs of editing.

Step 4

It stamps a verdict

Confirmed, False, or Under review. Anything uncertain goes to a human fact-checker.

Step 5

It replies

In the thread or the chat, in the voter's language, with the source and its date.

The same answer, in the language you think in.

Voice notes are how many Nigerians use WhatsApp. Confam understands spoken questions and replies with a voice note in the same language. Every translation is checked by native speakers before launch.

What Confam will never do.

These rules are built into the system, not left to the AI model's judgement.

Guess.

If no verified source supports an answer, Confam says so and passes the question to a human fact-checker.

Take sides.

It doesn't comment on parties or candidates, and it won't tell anyone how to vote.

Declare results.

Results come from INEC. Confam only points people to what INEC has published.

Sell your data.

It keeps only what it needs to reply, encrypts it, deletes it on a schedule, and follows the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

Reply uninvited.

It only answers when someone tags it, comments on its posts or messages it, as each platform's rules require.

Questions people ask first.

Who runs Confam?

Confam is proposed as an AI tool for INEC: the Commission would run it to counter election misinformation online, with independent fact-checking partners reviewing the hard cases. Until INEC adopts it, it is an independent, nonpartisan proposal and does not speak for the Commission.

What does INEC get from it?

A live picture of which false claims are spreading, on which platforms and in which languages, built from the questions voters send. INEC's team can spot a rumour early and publish a correction before it peaks.

How does it avoid wrong answers?

It can only answer from a curated library of verified sources: INEC publications and fact-checks published by recognised fact-checking organisations. When the library has no answer, it says so and passes the question to a person.

Could someone use it to push a party or candidate?

No. Questions about who to vote for are refused, it never comments on parties or candidates, and it never announces results. Those rules are enforced by the system itself.

What happens to my messages and phone number?

Only what's needed to reply is processed. Data is encrypted, deleted on a fixed schedule, never sold or shared, and handled in line with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

Which languages does it speak?

English, Nigerian Pidgin, Yorùbá, Hausa and Igbo at launch, by text and voice note. More Nigerian languages follow as the voice models and source library grow.
